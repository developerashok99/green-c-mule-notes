Observe here: we talked about connection pooling — do you remember? You can also use a reconnection strategy here. What is connection pooling? Generally, a request comes to the Salesforce connector, a connection is created, and through that connection we connect to the Salesforce system and send the request. A response comes back, the connection is closed, and the result passes to the next component. So for each request: create the connection, establish it, connect to Salesforce, send the request, get the response, close the connection, pass on.

What connection pooling says is: first create some connections and keep them ready, like a pool. Then when a request comes, it uses a connection already in the pool and goes fast. A normal scenario: you know a guest is coming to your house. Normally they come in through the gate and ring the bell; you're in the bedroom or on the first floor; after the bell rings you check and open the door; then they come in and sit. Since we know the guest is coming, I keep the door open. How many steps did I eliminate? The bell ring and the step of opening the door — time saved there. In the same way, this may seem small, but thousands of requests come to the system, and each opens and closes a connection. So it's important to keep connections ready. Latency means delay in the response; if I keep connections ready, performance improves — for example, if it takes 100 ms, it may take 90 ms; 10 ms saved due to connection pooling. If you want to improve performance, use connection pooling.
